📊 Project Documentation: Enterprise Sales & Profit Performance Dashboard🎯 Project OverviewThis repository contains an interactive Data Analytics Dashboard designed to monitor, analyze, and optimize business performance. It synthesizes complex transactional data into a single, cohesive interface, enabling stakeholders to evaluate revenue generation, profitability metrics, and distribution channels over a four-year horizon (2017 – 2020).🚀 Project PurposeThe primary objective of this project is to convert raw transactional records into actionable strategic insights. By analyzing the relationship between sales volume and actual profitability, this dashboard empowers business leaders to:Identify High-Value Regions: Pinpoint which geographic markets generate the most revenue versus which ones are the most cost-efficient.Monitor Growth Trajectories: Track year-over-year growth performance to understand macro business health.Optimize Sales Channels: Evaluate the revenue distribution between physical and digital storefronts to guide infrastructure investments.Audit Customer Accounts: Analyze corporate client profiles to discover which accounts yield the healthiest financial margins.🛠️ Dashboard Structure & Architecture[Year & Month Slicers] -> Global Interactive Filters

This indicates a high regional dependency for top-line growth.Volume Does Not Equal EfficiencyWhile Delhi NCR brings in the largest sum of money, it ranks low on profitability percentage (2.30%). Conversely, smaller markets like Surat (4.86%) and Patna (4.12%) are significantly more efficient at converting sales into actual profit.Slower Post-2018 ConsolidationThe business experienced a major revenue surge in 2018 (413.69M), followed by a minor drop in volume in 2019 (336.02M). However, operational efficiency improved in 2019, hitting a peak profit margin of 3.12%. A steep contraction in both volume and margin occurred in 2020, signaling a critical period requiring strategic operational changes.Core Dependency on Traditional RetailPhysical retail (Brick & Mortar) remains the foundational core of this business model, pulling in three-quarters (75.60%) of overall revenue. While digital sales (E-Commerce) hold a smaller share at 24.40%, they represent a modern avenue for potential low-overhead scaling.
